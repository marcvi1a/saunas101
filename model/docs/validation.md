# Validation targets for the ventilation model (agent E)

What the finished model must land near. Our room: 12 m³, floor 2.63 × 2.24 = 5.89 m², 2 bathers, outdoor 25 °C, ceiling sensor 90 °C, Ø125 openings, 10 mm door undercut. Switch words are lab.js's (`below` = Low, `bottom` = Floor, `riser` = Long duct, `half` = Short duct).

Readings marked "fig." were digitised by eye from the report's plots, ±3 °C. Where a source room differs from ours, the **difference between layouts** is the target, not the absolute value.

## Sources (short names used in the table)

- **VTT 1992**: Äikäs, E. & Holmberg, R. *Saunan lämpötilat ja ilmanvaihto* [Temperature and ventilation of the Finnish sauna]. VTT Tiedotteita 1431, Espoo 1992, 34 p. https://publications.vtt.fi/pdf/tiedotteet/1992/T1431.pdf
  - Lab sauna: 2 × 2 × 2 m (8 m³), Helo SKSM 80D 8 kW heater, thermostat 90 °C at head height (1.65 m), mechanical exhaust only.
  - Flow: 48 kg/h, about 11 l/s or 5 ACH (some runs at 96 kg/h).
  - Openings: open Ø160 holes. Inlets T1 0.15 m (under the heater), T2 0.51 m (behind it), T3 0.89 m (just above it), T4 1.45 m. Exhausts P1 0.15 m, P2 0.69 m (under the seat bench), P3 1.89 m.
  - Benches: foot bench 0.50 m, seat bench 0.90 m.
  - Tightness: door sealed, leakage 0.08/h at 5 Pa.
  - **The heights match ours:** T3 ≈ Above (0.98), T1 ≈ Low (0.12), P1 ≈ lower grille (0.25), P2 = upper grille (0.69), foot-bench air at 0.55 m = our upper-bench ankles.
- **VTT 2002**: Saari, M. et al. *Terveen saunan tekijät* [Elements of healthy sauna]. VTT Tiedotteita 2144, Espoo 2002, 60 p. + app. 47 p. https://sarjaweb.vtt.fi/pdf/tiedotteet/2002/T2144.pdf. Nine Helsinki saunas, measured in 2001.
- **D2**: Finnish building code D2 (1987), sauna rows. Quoted in VTT 1992 p. 7 and VTT 2002 Table 5, p. 30. The earlier version of this page said D2 was repealed with no sauna rule in the 2017 decree; that was not re-verified here.
- **Harvia**: support article "Ventilation in the sauna" (electric heater; the page refuses automated fetches, so its content was read through a search summary, moderate confidence), plus the Harvia 20 Pro manual §3.1.1 (2016, read directly).
- **Helo**: Himalaya installation manual §2.2, Fig. 1.
- **Sawo**: Taurus manual, "Air Ventilation", p. 11.
- **HUUM**: "Sauna ventilation: what are the most important considerations", huum.eu, 08.04.2022.
- **Tylö**: Sense Combi manual, English section, pp. 13–14.
- **Saunologia**: Angell, W. "How carbon dioxide affects sauna air quality", saunologia.fi, 13.03.2024.
- **Saunologia (Liikkanen)**: Liikkanen, L. A. "Saunan koneellinen ilmanvaihto", saunologia.fi, 14.05.2016, updated 29.05.2025. It gives D2 (2012) as 2 l/s per m² and at least 6 l/s, suggests 3–7 l/s per m², and says a gravity inlet above the stove "can turn into an exhaust when löyly is thrown".

## The five I trust most

1. **#3–#6, VTT 1992 foot-bench temperatures.** Controlled lab, same inlet and exhaust heights as ours, fan-driven. A high inlet over the heater warms the 0.55 m layer by 5–20 °C over a floor inlet, and the recommended layout's head-to-feet difference is 25–30 °C.
2. **#7–#8, VTT 1992 short circuit.** With a fan, a low inlet plus low exhaust needs 3–4× the flow to match a high inlet at head height. An exhaust under the seat bench (= our upper grille) removes the short circuit.
3. **#9, Saunologia's 680 ppm.** One bather, 14 m³, 11 l/s, a 15-minute round. A well-mixed transient at EN 16798-1's 20 L/h per person reproduces it within 3 %.
4. **#12, VTT 2002 S9.** Our volume, our recommended geometry, an exhaust-only fan, and the high inlet still ran backwards with 80–100 °C air leaving through it. The model must be able to do this with a weak fan or a leaky door.
5. **#1, the fan band.** D2, VTT 2002, Äikäs, Harvia, Saunologia and the site's own rule all land in 17–27 l/s for this room.
