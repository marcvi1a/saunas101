Construction Heater Ventilation

  • 15. Löyly is sauna

    Water thrown on the stones flashes into steam: löyly, the heat you feel. It rises to the ceiling, and only ventilation brings it down to the benches.

  • 16. Holes in the right places

    Inlet usually above the stones, exhaust on the opposite side under the lower bench, and drying vent high up that stays shut while bathing and opens after use.

  • 17. Electric heater needs a fan

    A wood stove draws air up its chimney and ventilates itself. An electric heater draws none: fit an exhaust fan of about 5 l/s per seat, never one on the inlet.

Most common layouts

    How the score is built. Breathing × löyly × heat, scaled from a sealed room (0) to the best layout (100). An open drying vent is for after the session.

    Breathing is the CO₂ at the upper bather's mouth: full at about 970 ppm, the best class of EN 16798-1, and zero at a sealed room's.

    Löyly is the share of the heater's rising, steam-carrying air that comes down to the benches instead of escaping at the ceiling.

    Heat is how much of the room's heat the bathers sit in, from ankles to mouth. A ventilated room that leaves the benches cold is not a sauna.

    The air is a pressure balance, not CFD: 125 mm openings, a door with 2 mm gaps and a 120 mm fan, scored running. A duct that starts cold can run backwards and is scored that way.
