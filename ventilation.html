Saunas 101

How a proper Finnish sauna works, so you can build your own.

Construction Heater Ventilation

  • 15. Löyly is sauna

    Water thrown on the stones flashes into steam: löyly, the heat you feel. It rises to the ceiling, and only ventilation brings it down to the benches.

  • 16. Holes in the right places

    Inlet usually above the stones, exhaust on the opposite side under the lower bench, and drying vent high up that stays shut while bathing and opens after use.

  • 17. Electric heater needs a fan

    A wood stove draws air up its chimney and ventilates itself. An electric heater draws none: fit an exhaust fan of about 5 l/s per seat, never one on the inlet.

Most common layouts

    How the score is built. Breathing × feet × löyly × heat, worked out from the physics of this 12 m³ room with 2 bathers, one on each bench, on a scale from a sealed room (0) to the best layout the room allows (100). An open drying vent can only lower a score: it is for after the session.

    Breathing is the CO₂ at the mouth of the bather on the upper bench once the air has settled: full at about 970 ppm, the best class of EN 16798-1, falling to nothing at the CO₂ of a sealed room, along the share of people who find the air stale.

    Löyly is the share of the heater's rising air, which carries the steam, that comes down to the benches instead of leaving at the ceiling through an open drying vent or the gap over the door.

    Heat is the share of the room's heat, 90 °C at the thermostat over 25 °C outside, that the bathers sit in, from their ankles to their mouths: a ventilated room that leaves the benches cold is not a sauna.

    Feet is the share of bathers untroubled by air moving at their ankles, from the ASHRAE 55 draught model stretched to sauna heat. In a hot room a breeze at the ankles bothers almost nobody, so it moves a score by 2 points at most; how warm the feet are is counted in heat.

    The air is a pressure balance, not CFD: warm room air against 25 °C outside, 125 mm inlet and exhaust, a well-fitted door with 2 mm gaps, a bare duct cooling outdoors, a 120 mm fan on its curve. The heater's plume and the inlet's jet decide how much fresh air reaches the bench. A duct that starts cold can run backwards and is scored that way, and the fan is scored running.
